## Executive Summary: Defining the Business Problem

This project focuses on maximizing the profitability of a direct marketing campaign for an international airline. The company is evaluating a marketing initiative where selected customers receive a complimentary first-class kit, accompanied by an offer to purchase a discounted flight to a new destination. The goal of the project is to develop a machine learning model that accurately identifies which customers should receive the offer in order to maximize the expected profit of the campaign.

**The Economic Parameters:**
*   **Cost of contacting a customer:** $15.20 (Cost of the kit and logistics).
*   **Net profit from a purchase:** $78.40 (After deducting the contact cost).

The model is based on an analysis of customer purchase history and behavior, and it integrates a text mining model for sentiment analysis derived from reviews left by some customers in the purchase history. The model's success is measured by the final bottom-line profit it yields for the company on new customer data.

## Part B: Model Application on Real Data and Recommendations for the Company

The second part of the project serves as the reality test for the winning model we built in the previous stage. The company provided us with a new database of 20,000 additional customers, along with 1,501 additional reviews. The goal of this stage is to produce individual recommendations for the company regarding each customer — whether it is worthwhile or not to contact them in a marketing campaign. The actual target variable indicating the true presence or absence of a purchase was hidden from us to test the true predictive capabilities of the model.

To ensure that our model (XGBoost) would produce predictions as effectively as it did during the development stages, we had to perform the exact same processing and preparation steps on the new data. 

1.  First, we used the pre-trained text mining model to extract sentiment scores from the new reviews. 
2.  Next, we performed the exact same feature engineering: deleting the noisy features we identified during the training phase, and creating Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) and Trend variables from the annual columns. 
3.  Once the new data perfectly matched the structure of the training data, we fed it into the model.

The output of the winning model was cut according to the strict economic decision threshold we calculated - **16.24%**. The final product was generated as the required `recommendations.csv` file, marking `1` in cases where contact is recommended or `0` when it is not, for each identifier in the database.

A stability check we conducted prior to submission showed that our model is ready for the real world: The distribution of predictions, the percentage of customers flagged for marketing contact, and the estimated expected profit on the new rollout data were almost identical to those in the training phase, with a negligible deviation of only $61 from the expected profit. The data indicates that the model does not suffer from overfitting, providing high confidence in the model's real-world performance.
